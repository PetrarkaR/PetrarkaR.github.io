What am I looking at?

The blue line is a sine wave over one second. Each pink dot is one measurement. The pink connecting lines are a visual guide, not a reconstructed signal.

To recover a sine wave, the sample rate must be greater than twice its frequency. At or below that boundary, different waves can give the same samples. That ambiguity is called aliasing. Try 7 Hz with 8 samples per second: it looks like a much slower wave.

This demo starts the wave at zero phase. At exactly twice the frequency, every sample lands at a zero crossing—an especially striking way to lose the signal.
